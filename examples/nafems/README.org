#+TITLE: NAFEMS benchmarks, as parts you can open
#+OPTIONS: toc:2

* What this is

Nine of the NAFEMS benchmarks. Eight are a part you can open in mep's CAD
pane plus a script that runs the whole analysis on it and compares the
answer against a reference; the ninth, FV42, is a script that explains
why it cannot be a part at all.

They are the counterpart of =src/fem_nafems_test.cpp=, not a copy of it.
That file builds its meshes by hand in C++ and can reach anything the
library can, so it proves the *solver*. These prove the **surface**:
every one of them uses only =mep.part_*= and =mep.fem_*=, the thirty-four
methods Part K binds into Lua and routes over the agent socket. Where a
benchmark cannot be stated through that surface, the script says so and
stops rather than quietly stating a different problem.

* Running one

From the repository root, either way round:

#+begin_src sh
./build/native/mep --run-lua examples/nafems/le10.lua
#+end_src

or, inside a running mep:

#+begin_example
:source examples/nafems/le10.lua
#+end_example

=just nafems= runs all of them and prints a table. Several take a minute
or two and FV41 takes several: a modal analysis of ten thousand
hexahedra is not quick, and a free-free one needs more iterations than a
restrained one.

* Opening the geometry

Every =.mepcad= file here is a **feature tree** -- sketches and the
extrudes and revolves built on them, with no geometry in the file at all.
Open one in mep and you get an editable part, not a frozen body:

#+begin_example
:e examples/nafems/le10.mepcad
#+end_example

They are generated by =mep-nafems-examples= (=just nafems-geometry=),
which rebuilds every tree it writes and checks its volume against the
closed form, so a geometry that has quietly stopped being the benchmark's
says so there rather than in a frequency six steps later. All eight were
opened in a running mep over the agent socket and rebuilt in the pane:
LE10 comes back as =ellipses= then =plate=, LE7 and FV41 as =section=,
=tube=, =quadrant=, =quarter=.

FV42 has no =.mepcad=. A hollow sphere is a revolve whose profile
necessarily touches the axis at both poles, and mep's revolve refuses
that by name; =fv42.lua= builds it from two =part.sphere= primitives and
a difference instead, and writes =fv42.step= for the CAD pane.

* The films

Each script writes a =.mov= and a =.png= into =examples/nafems/film/=
(override with =NAFEMS_OUT=). The films are Motion-JPEG in a QuickTime
container, written by mep's own encoder, and they open in mep's video
pane:

#+begin_example
:e examples/nafems/film/fv52.mov
#+end_example

A static result is filmed with the load going on and coming off again --
a linear result scales exactly with the load, so every intermediate frame
is a real answer to a real load case rather than an interpolation, and a
full cycle loops seamlessly. A mode is filmed over a cycle of its own
period, at an amplitude chosen from the geometry, because a mode shape is
an eigenvector and has no amplitude of its own.

They are not checked in: they are build output, and a repository is not a
video host.

|---------+----------------------------------+---------------------------------------+-------|
| le1     | elliptic membrane, plane stress  | sigma_yy at the hole, 92.7 MPa        | blocked in the mesher |
| le7     | thick cylinder, internal pressure| Lame's exact stress through the wall  | runs  |
| le10    | thick elliptic plate, pressure   | sigma_yy at the hole, -5.38 MPa       | runs  |
| fv5     | deep simply supported beam       | Timoshenko's first three modes        | runs  |
| fv22    | clamped rhombic plate            | above the clamped square              | blocked in the mesher |
| fv32    | cantilevered tapered membrane    | above both untapered strips           | blocked in the mesher |
| fv41    | free hollow cylinder             | the ring, corrected for axial inertia | runs  |
| fv42    | thick hollow sphere              | the traction-free determinant's root  | blocked in the kernel |
| fv52    | simply supported square plate    | Mindlin's first four modes            | runs  |

Four of the benchmarks in =plans/NAFEMS_PLAN.md= are not here at all --
T1, T3, T4 and LE11. All four are thermal, and the agent surface has no
thermal study: =fem_thermal.cpp= implements conduction, convection,
radiation and the transient integrator, and none of it is bound. That is
a missing binding rather than a missing capability, and it is the largest
single gap in this surface.

* What the examples found

Writing these was meant to be a demonstration. It turned into a test, and
it failed in five places -- every one of them in mep rather than in the
benchmarks. The full write-up is in =plans/NAFEMS_PLAN.md=; in short:

1. **A pressure on a swept mesh acted the wrong way.** The sweeper stored
   its profile's rim edges as *sorted* index pairs, so the side faces
   were wound in or out according to how the nodes happened to be
   numbered. LE7's bore moved inward under internal pressure. Fixed.
2. **The feature tree judged a part in metres by a tolerance meant for
   millimetres.** An extruded ellipse 3.25 m across follows its own edge
   to about a micron -- three parts in ten million, and entirely
   respectable -- and failed validation outright. Fixed: the tolerance is
   now relative to the body.
3. **=part.faces= reported a point that is not on the face.** It is the
   surface evaluated at the middle of its own parameter domain, and a
   face is a trimmed piece of a surface: LE10's outer edge reported a
   point on the far side of the ellipse. Picking "the face nearest here"
   silently returned the wrong one. Fixed by adding a centroid and a
   bounding box taken from the face's own tessellation.
4. **The surface mesher fails on a planar face that is not a
   rectangle.** A rhombus and a trapezoid are both enough; a square and a
   rectangle are fine. This blocks FV22 and FV32 outright and is the
   largest of the five. Not fixed.
5. **The tetrahedral mesher does not terminate on a thin slab.** LE1's
   part is 0.05 m thick and 6.5 m across. Not fixed.

And one that is a gap rather than a defect: a **hollow sphere cannot be
made through this surface at all**. A revolve refuses a profile that
touches its axis, which a sphere's does at both poles; the boolean
refuses to cut a face whose parameterisation has a pole; and =part.shell=
handles planar faces only. FV42 is blocked for that reason and not for a
meshing one, and its script derives the exact reference anyway.

Two smaller ones: the stress components had no compact field names, so
=stress_yy= -- the *target quantity* of two of these benchmarks -- was
rejected; and =ExtrudeEnd::Symmetric= was documented as "distance each
way" when it is the total.

And three things that were missing rather than wrong, now on the surface:
sweeping to hexahedra (=fem.mesh method="sweep"=), which was built in
Part G.6 and reachable only from C++; edges and edge supports
(=part.edges=, =part.edge_at=), without which a simple support has to be
faked as a built-in end; and =fem.nodes=, without which a mode cannot be
identified by what it is.
