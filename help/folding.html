Folds

The keys

All on the z prefix, as in Vim. A fold at the cursor means one whose range covers the cursor's row; where a nest of them does, one level opens from the outside in and closes from the inside out, so repeated presses walk through the nest a level at a time. za follows the same rule: on a closed fold it opens the outermost closed one -- the fold you are actually looking at -- and otherwise closes the innermost open one.

The recursive forms (zA, zO, zC) act on the whole nest the cursor's fold belongs to: the folds around it and the ones inside it. From a closed fold's summary line that matters, since everything nested within it begins below the row the cursor is on.

KeyDoes
zatoggle the fold at the cursor
zAtoggle that fold and the whole nest it belongs to
zoopen one level at the cursor
zOopen the whole nest at the cursor
zcclose one level at the cursor
zCclose the whole nest at the cursor
zvopen just enough to see the line the cursor is on
zRopen every fold in the buffer
zMclose every fold
zrone fold level less
zmone fold level more

Closing a fold the cursor is inside moves the cursor to that fold's summary line, rather than leaving it on a hidden row.

Pause on z without typing a second key and a panel of every fold command appears along the bottom of the window -- which-key, for the z prefix. It is only a reminder: the keys still resolve immediately, so typing zc at speed never puts it on screen and nothing it shows can swallow a keystroke. In Visual mode it lists what Visual mode actually has, which is zf.

o and O on a closed fold open the new line outside it -- after the last collapsed row, or before the first -- rather than dropping what you type into the middle of a region you folded away.

Making and unmaking them

KeyDoes
zf(Visual) fold the selected lines, closed
[count]zFfold count lines from the cursor down, closed
zddelete the fold at the cursor
zDdelete every fold at the cursor
zEdelete every fold in the buffer
zxrebuild the computed folds now, and do zv

zf takes whichever lines the selection touches -- charwise or linewise, it folds whole lines either way -- and needs at least two of them, as does zF. A fold either of them makes is yours, not a provider's: none of the recomputes below clear it, and it follows the text as you edit around it.

A fold follows its text through every edit, including the ones that replace the buffer wholesale: undo and redo put a fold back exactly where the text went back to, :m and :t carry it along, and so do the commands that rewrite a region (an Org subtree move, a TODO cycle, a property drawer) and anything a script or an agent splices in. Before that was true, deleting four lines and undoing it left every fold below the deletion four rows above its own text, and the drift compounded with each further undo.

Folds nest, and never cross. Folding the same lines twice gives you one fold rather than two stacked copies, and folding a range that partly overlaps an existing fold widens that fold to cover both rather than leaving two ranges that each hide part of the other -- a pair like that has no summary line to open the second one from, so it reads as a fold that will not open. The same tidy-up re-fits every fold to the file after a change that replaced its lines outright (an undo, a :e!, a reload), so a range can never point past the end of the buffer.

zd and zD remove the fold itself, not just its collapsed state. One that a provider owns can be rebuilt by that provider's next recompute -- the same caveat vim's own zd carries outside foldmethod=manual. zE is the exception: it stops the automatic rebuild as well, so the buffer stays unfolded until zx asks for the folds back.

Turning folding off

KeyDoes
zifolding off and on ('foldenable')
znoff: every fold opens, and nothing collapses
zNon: every fold that was collapsed goes back as it was

Folding off opens everything without losing it -- zN (or zi again) puts each fold back exactly as you left it. Any command that closes a fold turns folding back on first, as in Vim.

Moving between them

KeyDoes
zjto the start of the next fold
zkto the end of the previous fold

What a folded line looks like

A closed fold keeps its own first line -- syntax highlighting and all -- rather than replacing it with a synthetic placeholder, and marks itself four ways: a tinted band across the row, an accent rule down its left edge, an accent + in the gutter, and ... N lines after the text saying how much is behind it. An open fold's start row shows a - in the gutter. Clicking either marker toggles that fold without moving the cursor.

The band and rule are derived from the theme's accent color, so a fold reads the same way in every palette.

Where folds come from

SourceFor
Treesittercode, via :MepSyntaxFold
headline depthOrg and Markdown
\{\{\{ and \}\}\}marker folds, in any filetype
zf in Visual modeone you make by hand, any filetype

Org and marker folds are recomputed lazily -- right before a fold command touches the buffer, rather than on every keystroke -- so they are always current without costing anything while you type.

Org has its own cycling on :MepOrgCycle, which is the more natural way to move through an outline.

Folds across sessions

Folds are saved in the project session file, next to the layout, tabs and cursor positions, and come back when the project is reopened. The save is the same debounced one the layout gets -- half a second after you fold something, not only on the way out -- so a session that ends in a crash or a kill still has them.

KindSaved as
a fold you made (zf / zF)the fold itself
a provider's fold you collapsedthe collapse, not the fold
a provider's fold left opennothing

A provider's fold (an Org headline, a \{\{\{ marker, a treesitter block) is rebuilt from the text next time anyway, so there is nothing worth saving but the fact that you had closed it -- which comes back matched by start row, the same way a recompute preserves it inside a session. A fold you made by hand exists nowhere but in mep, so it is saved whole.

Two things to know:

  • Folds come back whether or not a layout does. Opening a project (from the dashboard, or mep --project <path>) restores its whole saved layout; a bare mep file.txt restores no layout at all, by design -- but it still finds that file folded the way you left it, because a fold belongs to the buffer rather than to the pane.
  • They come back whenever the file is opened, not just at startup. A file reached later in the session -- from the file tree, :e, the picker, a new split -- finds its folds waiting the moment its buffer is created.
  • A file you never opened keeps its folds. The session file is rewritten with what was saved for it last time rather than only what happened to be on screen, so working on one file for an afternoon does not quietly unfold every other one.
  • A saved range that no longer fits the file (it was edited elsewhere in between, or shrank) is dropped rather than applied to the wrong lines.
