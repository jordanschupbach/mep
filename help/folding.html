Folds

The keys

All on the z prefix, as in Vim. A fold at the cursor means one whose range covers the cursor's row; where a nest of them does, one level opens from the outside in and closes from the inside out, so repeated presses walk through the nest a level at a time.

KeyDoes
zatoggle the fold at the cursor
zAtoggle it and everything nested in it
zoopen one level at the cursor
zOopen every fold at the cursor
zcclose one level at the cursor
zCclose every fold at the cursor
zvopen just enough to see the line the cursor is on
zRopen every fold in the buffer
zMclose every fold
zrone fold level less
zmone fold level more

Closing a fold the cursor is inside moves the cursor to that fold's summary line, rather than leaving it on a hidden row.

Making and unmaking them

KeyDoes
zf(Visual) fold the selected lines, closed
[count]zFfold count lines from the cursor down, closed
zddelete the fold at the cursor
zDdelete every fold at the cursor
zEdelete every fold in the buffer
zxrebuild the computed folds now, and do zv

zf takes whichever lines the selection touches -- charwise or linewise, it folds whole lines either way -- and needs at least two of them, as does zF. A fold either of them makes is yours, not a provider's: none of the recomputes below clear it, and it follows the text as you edit around it.

zd and zD remove the fold itself, not just its collapsed state. One that a provider owns can be rebuilt by that provider's next recompute -- the same caveat vim's own zd carries outside foldmethod=manual. zE is the exception: it stops the automatic rebuild as well, so the buffer stays unfolded until zx asks for the folds back.

Turning folding off

KeyDoes
zifolding off and on ('foldenable')
znoff: every fold opens, and nothing collapses
zNon: every fold that was collapsed goes back as it was

Folding off opens everything without losing it -- zN (or zi again) puts each fold back exactly as you left it. Any command that closes a fold turns folding back on first, as in Vim.

Moving between them

KeyDoes
zjto the start of the next fold
zkto the end of the previous fold

A folded region shows a marker in the gutter column, beside the line number: - for an open fold, + for a closed one. Clicking it toggles that fold without moving the cursor.

Where folds come from

SourceFor
Treesittercode, via :MepSyntaxFold
headline depthOrg and Markdown
\{\{\{ and \}\}\}marker folds, in any filetype
zf in Visual modeone you make by hand, any filetype

Org and marker folds are recomputed lazily -- right before a fold command touches the buffer, rather than on every keystroke -- so they are always current without costing anything while you type.

Org has its own cycling on :MepOrgCycle, which is the more natural way to move through an outline.

From Lua

lua Copy
mep.fold_create(start_row, end_row)
mep.fold_toggle(row)
mep.fold_clear_provider()

A feature that computes its own folds registers them with fold_create and clears its previous set with fold_clear_provider.
