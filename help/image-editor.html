The image editor

Opening it

Press e while viewing an image. Esc returns to the plain viewer.

Layers and undo history are kept per buffer, so Esc then e again resumes exactly where you left off -- leaving the editor is not the same as closing it.

The layout

A two-column tool sidebar on the left, the canvas in the middle, a Layers panel on the right. A thin toolbar above the canvas has the brush size and recent colours; the foreground and background swatches sit at the bottom of the tool sidebar. A menubar across the top has File, Edit, Layer and View.

With a selection active, Delete or Backspace clears its pixels and leaves the selection in place. Edit → Deselect drops it.

Layers

The Layers panel adds, deletes, duplicates, reorders, renames and hides layers, and sets each one's opacity. Click an already-selected layer's name to rename it.

Saving

:w flattens the visible layers and writes a real PNG.

Procedural textures

For a generated texture rather than a drawn one, the headless generators are far quicker than painting: wood, turned wood, marble, noise, gradients and checkerboards, plus a blur. They fill the active layer in one step.

Turned wood is the one to use for anything wrapped around a cylinder -- a plain wood cross-section spirals visibly when mapped onto one.

Not implemented

Worth knowing so you do not go looking: free transform, crop, canvas resize/resample, flip and rotate, anti-aliased brush edges, a text tool, blend modes beyond Normal, layer masks and adjustment operations are all absent.
