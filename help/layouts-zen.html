Layouts, zoom and zen

Rearranging every pane at once

:MepLayout {kind} rebuilds the current tab's split tree, keeping the same panes and the same buffers but arranging them differently. It is the fast way out of a layout that grew organically into a mess.

KindArrangement
master-leftthe first pane down the left, the rest stacked right
master-rightthe first pane down the right
master-topthe first pane across the top
master-bottomthe first pane across the bottom
gridan even grid
grid-ha grid, filling horizontally
grid-va grid, filling vertically
spiraleach pane splitting the remaining space in turn

master-left with a wide editor and a stack of terminals beside it is the common one. With one pane the command does nothing.

Zoom

<leader>zz (:MepPaneZoom) makes the current pane fill the tab, hiding the others, and pressing it again restores the layout. Useful for reading something wide without losing your arrangement.

mod1+m is the related key: instead of hiding the other panes it shrinks them to the minimum, so they stay visible at the edges. Use zz to concentrate, mod1+m to keep context.

Zen mode

:MepZen strips the interface back to the text: no tab bar, no sidebars, no status line. Run it again to bring them back.

Pair it with :set nonumber norelativenumber and a wide textwidth for prose.

Scratch space

<leader>bs (:MepScratch) opens a scratch buffer -- a real editable buffer with no file behind it. Combined with a split, it is the quickest place to paste something, reshape it and throw it away.

Font size

Ctrl+Shift+= and Ctrl+Shift+- grow and shrink the font in any mode. The window opens maximized, and both the native window and the wasm canvas resize the editor to whatever space they are given.

Saving a layout

Layouts are not saved by name, but they do not need to be: each project's whole arrangement -- workspaces, tabs, split trees, open files, cursors and terminals -- is written to disk half a second after any structural change and restored when the project is next opened.

So the arrangement you leave is the arrangement you come back to, without naming it. :wssave and :wsrestore do it by hand.
