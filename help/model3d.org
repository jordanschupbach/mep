* Opening a scene

=.obj=, =.gltf=, =.glb=, =.iqm=, =.vox= and =.m3d= open straight in the
modeler. =:Model3DNew= starts an empty scene.

=.blend= needs a real Blender on =$PATH=: mep converts it through Blender's
own command line, on a background job, so opening one never blocks. The pane
shows "Converting..." until it finishes.

* Building a scene

Primitives are =cube=, =sphere=, =cylinder=, =cone=, =plane=, =torus= and
=wedge=.

*Check the pivot before stacking parts.* Cylinder, cone and wedge are
base-pivoted rather than centred, so placing one at =y = 0= sits it on the
floor while a sphere at =y = 0= is half-buried. The modeler's
=primitive_info= reports each one's pivot and dimensions.

For a turned form no primitive can express -- a bottle, a table leg, a chess
piece -- a *lathe* revolves an ordered radius/height profile around the Y
axis into a welded mesh with real UVs. A profile ending at radius zero needs
no cap; it is already a point.

* Materials

A material is a colour plus roughness and metallic, feeding a real if
simplified metallic-roughness model: one fixed key light, no shadows and no
reflections, but low roughness with high metallic genuinely reads as shiny
metal against high roughness with low metallic for matte wood or stone.

Roughness and metallic are *sparse*: omitting one leaves its current value
rather than resetting it to zero.

Textures fill the albedo, normal, roughness and metallic slots. An albedo
texture is tinted by the object's own colour.

* Editing geometry

Beyond transforms, the modeler does real mesh editing: move, delete and
merge vertices, merge-by-distance, recalculate normals, subdivide, extrude,
inset, dissolve, add vertices, make faces, flip normals, and list triangles
to discover connectivity.

Two things that save time:

- =merge_by_distance= right after adding a primitive. The generators emit
  duplicate vertices at every shared corner, and unwelded faces extrude
  independently rather than as one surface.
- =flip_normals= when geometry looks inside out, which is the usual result
  of building a face with its corners in the wrong order.

There is no edge or face *selection*: an operation treats "the vertices
fully covering a triangle" as a face.

* Organising

Grouping and parenting are purely organisational -- a parent's transform is
never composed into a child's. The gizmos cascade to descendants in the UI,
but setting a transform programmatically does not.

Symmetry helpers do the repetitive work: mirror a duplicate across an axis,
or make a radial array for fins and spokes.

* Animation and output

Objects and the camera take keyframes, and a scene renders to a PNG or to a
video. =render_to_image= captures just the scene, unlike a window
screenshot.

=:w= saves.

* Driving it

The modeler has a full scripting surface -- =mep.model_*= from Lua, and the
same operations over the agent protocol -- so a scene can be built,
inspected, rendered and saved without touching the mouse. That is the
intended way to use it for anything repetitive.

=MEP_AGENT_API.md= has the complete reference and a worked example.
