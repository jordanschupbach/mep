Motions

Why motions matter twice over

A motion moves the cursor. It also describes a range for an operator: w moves to the next word, and dw deletes as far as w would have moved. Everything on this page therefore does double duty.

Within a line

KeyMoves to
h lone character left or right
0column zero
^the first non-blank character
$the end of the line
w Wthe start of the next word (W: whitespace-separated)
b Bthe start of the previous word
e Ethe end of the current or next word
ge gEthe end of the previous word
%the matching bracket

Between lines

KeyMoves to
j kone line down or up
{ }the previous or next blank line (paragraph)
ggthe first line
Gthe last line, or to a count's line
H M Lthe highest, middle or lowest visible line
:42line 42

j and k move by buffer line, not by visual row, so a long soft-wrapped line is one press either way.

Finding a character

f{char} jumps to the next occurrence of that character on the line, t{char} to just before it; F and T are the backward versions. ; repeats the last such search, =,= repeats it in the opposite direction.

These are the fastest way to give an operator a precise target: dt) deletes up to the next closing parenthesis, =ct"= changes up to the next quote.

Counts

Any motion takes a leading count, meaning "do it that many times".

TypedEffect
5jdown five lines
3wforward three words
10Ggo to line 10
d2wdelete two words
3dddelete three lines

A half-typed count appears in the status line, along with any pending operator or register prefix, so you can always see what mep is waiting for.

Jumping to what you can see

Press s and start typing the text you are aiming at. Matches across every visible text pane narrow as you type, everything else dims, and each match gets a one-key label drawn after it -- press that key to jump there, focusing another pane if that is where the match is.

  • A query that narrows to exactly one match jumps immediately.
  • Enter jumps to the nearest match.
  • Backspace edits the query, Esc cancels.
  • An all-lowercase query matches case-insensitively.

Labels never reuse a character that could extend the query at some live match, so a keypress is never ambiguous. Ctrl-O comes back afterwards.

Coming back

Ctrl-O and Ctrl-I walk backward and forward through the jumplist, which records searches, gg=/=G and mark jumps. It is per-pane and can span buffers. `` returns to where you were before the last big jump.
