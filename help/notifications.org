* Toasts

Most things mep wants to tell you arrive as a toast in the corner: a git
push finishing, a formatter failing, a language server attaching. Toasts
dismiss themselves after a few seconds, the more serious ones sticking
around longer.

| Level | Dismisses after |
|-------+-----------------|
| error | 8 seconds       |
| warn  | 6 seconds       |
| info  | 4 seconds       |
| debug | 3 seconds       |

* When you missed one

That is what the history panel is for. =<leader>nn= (=:MepNotifyPanel=, or
the Notifications button in the tab bar) lists everything that has been
raised this session, including toasts that have already faded.

| Command             | Does                                |
|---------------------+-------------------------------------|
| =:MepNotifyPanel=   | open the history panel              |
| =:MepNotifyDismiss= | clear the toasts currently on screen |
| =:MepNotifyClear=   | empty the history                   |

=:MepNotifyDismiss= is the one you want when a stack of toasts is covering
what you are reading; it leaves the history intact.

* From Lua

#+begin_src lua
mep.notify('Build finished')
mep.notify('No formatter for this filetype', 'warn')
mep.notify('Could not reach the server', 'error')
#+end_src

Lua's own =print()= is routed through =mep.notify= as well, so a =print= in
your config or in a =:lua= one-liner shows up as a toast rather than
vanishing into a terminal mep does not have.

#+begin_src lua
:lua print(mep.line_count())
#+end_src

That makes =print= a perfectly good debugging tool here -- and it means a
chatty =print= in a frame hook will bury you in toasts, so keep those out of
=mep.on_frame=.
