Presentations

Opening

.pptx (PowerPoint) and .odp (LibreOffice Impress) open in a slide editor: the slide drawn to scale on a canvas, a strip of slide thumbnails down the left, and a toolbar across the top. Opening a path that does not exist yet starts a new deck there; :PresNew starts one with no name.

Working on a slide

Click a shape to select it, drag it to move it, and drag one of its eight handles to resize it (a picture keeps its shape from a corner). Double-click a shape's text, or press Enter, to type into it; Esc stops typing.

While typing, Enter starts a new paragraph, Tab / Shift-Tab indent a list item, and Ctrl-B / Ctrl-I / Ctrl-U style the paragraph the caret is in. Without typing, styling applies to all of the selected shape's text.

The toolbar has the same actions, plus alignment and colour rows for a shape's fill, its text, its outline, and the slide's background.

Slides are drawn in the editor's theme colours by default, as PDFs and pictures are: each colour's lightness is placed on the gradient from the theme's text colour to its background, so a white slide with black text takes the theme's background and text colours. Ctrl-R (or the toolbar's half-circle button) switches to the slides' own colours and back. This is only how the editor draws them -- the file keeps its own colours.

Maths

Equations are typeset by mep's own maths engine, the one the editor uses for LaTeX elsewhere: PowerPoint's equations (Office Math), Impress formula objects, and the maths mep's own mepml exports write.

Key or commandDoes
:PresMath texa new equation (or, with an equation selected, its new TeX)
Ctrl-Mwhile typing: maths at the caret, typing carries on after it
Enter, double-click an equationits TeX on the command line, to change

An equation is one character to the caret: arrows step over it and Backspace deletes it whole.

In a .pptx equations are Office equations, with a picture of each displayed equation (and inline maths as text) for programs that cannot show those -- LibreOffice Impress among them. In an .odp a displayed equation is a formula object, while inline maths in a text box is written as text (Impress text boxes cannot hold a formula), so it reads back as text.

Saving

:w writes the deck in the format its name gives it, so :w talk.odp on a .pptx is also how one converts. mep reads what a file inherits from its masters, layouts and theme and writes every shape out on its own slide: the deck looks the same in PowerPoint and Impress afterwards, but its placeholders become ordinary text boxes. What mep has no model for -- animations, transitions, charts, SmartArt, speaker notes' formatting -- is not kept. A picture mep cannot draw (an EMF or WMF metafile) shows as a labelled box and is saved unchanged.

Scripting

mep.pres_info() describes the current slide (its shapes, their positions in EMU -- 914400 to the inch -- and their text). mep.pres_goto(n), mep.pres_select(n), mep.pres_add_slide(), mep.pres_add_shape(kind), mep.pres_set_text(text), mep.pres_set_geometry(x, y, w, h), mep.pres_style("b"), mep.pres_align("center"), mep.pres_color("fill", "#6b8afd"), mep.pres_math(tex), mep.pres_theme(on), mep.pres_undo() and mep.pres_redo() do what the keys do.
